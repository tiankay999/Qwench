# Qwench: the business

**Tagline:** Qwench fraud before it sends.
**Track:** Transparency and Digital Infrastructure.

## 1. The idea in brief

Qwench is **Ghana's fraud bureau**. Banks use credit bureaus to know who they're lending to. Qwench helps them, and their customers, know who they're paying.

Its core product is **Know Your Payee**. Before a payment goes through, a bank or mobile money app checks the recipient's number, account or wallet against two things: schemes regulators have named, and fraud institutions have confirmed. If there's a match, the institution can warn the customer, review the payment, or call to confirm it before the money moves.

**Free for the public. Paid for by institutions.**

## 2. The problem

**Once money leaves, it's almost always gone.** Banks recovered only about 5% of fraud losses in 2025, according to the Ghana Association of Banks.

- There were **24,778 financial fraud cases** in 2025, mostly at payment providers (Bank of Ghana).
- **The SEC named 23 unlicensed platforms** in one notice in July 2026.
- **Ghana's bankers publicly called for a shared fraud intelligence platform** on 23 October 2026.

**The information exists, but it's scattered:**

| Who | What they see | What they miss |
|---|---|---|
| **The customer** | The offer | Whether regulators have warned about it |
| **Banks and MoMo operators** | Their own payments | The offer behind the payment, and activity on other networks |
| **Regulators** | Complaints, often late | What's spreading now |

**The cost to institutions goes beyond the loss.** Every fraud case adds calls, investigations, disputes and customers who leave. That raises the **cost to serve** each customer.

## 3. The solution

| Part | What it does | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reviewed record** | Regulator notices, licences and sandbox firms, plus the names, websites, numbers and wallets linked to them. Every fact is sourced and checked by a second person. | Foundation for everything |
| **Know Your Payee API** | Checks a recipient at the moment of payment, including inside USSD flows | Banks, MoMo operators, payment firms |
| **Daily feed** | New and changed records for institutions' fraud systems | Fraud teams |
| **Bank console** | Lookups, cases, reports and signals in one place | Fraud and compliance staff |
| **Free public check** | Send a link, number, screenshot or whole message on WhatsApp, Telegram or the web | The public |
| **Early warning** | Shows what people are starting to ask about, often before complaints arrive | Institutions and regulators |
| **Shared layer** | Partners pool what schemes use, never customer data | Founding partners and members |
| **Cases** | Opens a case when concern passes a threshold, checks claims without contacting the suspect, and sends evidence to the authorities | Institutions and regulators |
| **Merchant registry** | Businesses register official numbers, so fake ones can be spotted | Merchants and their customers |
| **Brand Watch** | Alerts firms when their name appears on fake websites, listings or social accounts | Banks, merchants, virtual asset firms |

**There's no app to download.** Qwench works in the chats people already use, and inside the banking and MoMo apps they already have.

## 4. How it works

**Example: Know Your Payee**
1. A customer enters a number to send GH₵800.
2. The app checks it with Qwench.
3. The number is linked to a scheme named in an SEC notice.
4. The app warns the customer before they enter their PIN.
5. For a large payment to a new recipient, the bank can call to confirm.

**Most payments are untouched.** The warning appears only when there's a match, so normal customers see no extra step.

**Example: a fake business number**
1. A customer finds a number advertised as a well-known restaurant.
2. Qwench knows it isn't one of that restaurant's registered numbers.
3. Anyone paying it sees: *"This number has been advertised as [business], but it isn't one of its registered numbers."*

**Example: early warning**
1. Checks of a new scheme jump from 4 to 187 in a week (illustrative).
2. A person reviews the spike and opens a case.
3. Banks get the case for internal review, and the CSA and SEC get an evidence pack.
4. If a regulator publishes a notice, customers can be warned everywhere.

## 5. Where the data comes from

| Level | Source | What it allows |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Regulators and sanctions lists | Customer warnings |
| **2** | Fraud confirmed by partners, and reviewed impersonation | Partner-confirmed: internal review, or warnings where partners agree. Reviewed impersonation: shown publicly |
| **3** | Independent reports with evidence | Internal review |
| **4** | A rise in checks | Watch only |
| **Not shown** | A single public report | Opens a review only |

**Available today:** public regulator notices, foreign regulators' lists, and what people check or forward, including TikTok and Telegram posts.

**Available after partnerships:** confirmed fraud numbers from MTN, other operators and banks. Regulator notices rarely list payment numbers, so **partners are essential**.

**Not used:** Truecaller data, automated scraping, reviews and comments.

## 6. Our rules

1. **We report evidence. We don't give verdicts.** No public scores, no "safe", no "scam" in our own words.
2. **We never give investment advice.**
3. **We never block a payment.** The institution decides.
4. **We never hold money.** Qwench won't become a wallet, because that would need a licence and make us a competitor to our partners.
5. **The crowd can make us look, but it can't make us warn.** Only regulators and confirmed data reach customers.
6. **Nobody can pay for a better result** or hide a warning.
7. **We never contact suspects.** Investigation belongs to the authorities.
8. **We store what was checked, not who checked it.**

## 9. Why institutions would choose Qwench

- **It lowers cost to serve** by stopping fraud before it reaches the contact centre.
- **It gives first movers an edge.** As with the move to chip cards, the first institutions set the standard, and others follow.
- **It sees across networks.** Schemes move between networks and banks, and no single institution sees all of it.
- **It adds to existing systems.** Like a credit bureau, it feeds their fraud tools rather than replacing them.

## 10. Competition

| Who | What they do | How Qwench relates |
|---|---|---|
| **CSA 292, MTN 419** | Take reports and calls | **Partners:** Qwench sends people to them and strengthens the data |
| **SEC portal** | Lists licensed firms | **Partner:** Qwench carries its data to the point of payment |
| **Bank fraud tools** | Monitor a bank's own transactions | **Complementary:** they don't see the offer |
| **Chainalysis, TRM, Elliptic** | Trace activity on the blockchain | **Complementary:** Qwench covers what happens off it |
| **World-Check** | Sanctions and high-risk screening | **Complementary:** little Ghana-specific notice data, based on what we found |
| **Bolster, Netcraft** | Brand protection | **Competitors for Brand Watch:** Qwench is local and cheaper |
| **Google and AI answers** | Answer general questions | Weak on new schemes and MoMo numbers; no audit trail |
| **Truecaller** | Crowd-based spam labels | Different data; a possible customer |
| **Doing nothing** | What most people do | **The biggest competitor** |

**"Why won't MTN build this?"** It can build for MTN. But Telecel, AT and the banks won't share fraud data with their biggest competitor. A shared system needs a neutral operator. That's why we want MTN as a founding partner.

**The real risk:** the bankers' association or Bank of Ghana builds the shared platform themselves. Our response is to offer Qwench as its investment-offer and public layer.

## 11. Regulators and the law

**Regulators are sources, not endorsers.** We ask them for two things:
1. Registers and warnings in a machine-readable format.
2. Recognition as a data-sharing partner, so institutions can contribute with confidence.

**In return,** they get an early-warning report, and their notices reach the point of payment.

| Law | What it means for Qwench |
|---|---|
| **VASP Act, 2025 (Act 1154)** | No advice; get the SEC's view before running alert channels |
| **Securities Industry Act (Act 929)** | Report SEC notices accurately; never judge a firm |
| **Data Protection Act (Act 843)** | Register; collect as little as possible; offer an appeal route |
| **Cybersecurity Act (Act 1038)** | Confirm with the CSA whether a licence applies |
| **Banks and SDIs Act (Act 930)** | Scheme details only, never customer records |
| **AML Act (Act 1044)** | Member reports are never public, to avoid tipping off |
| **Payment Systems Act (Act 987)** | Outside its licensing because we hold no money, but operators will vet us |
| **Copyright and defamation** | Regulators' originals only; every claim sourced |

**A Ghanaian lawyer needs to review this before launch.**

## 12. Going to market

**In order:**
1. **The CSA and SEC:** credibility and data.
2. **MTN as founding partner:** a 90-day pilot.
3. **One bank:** a free feed trial, then a case study.
4. **Sandbox firms:** a free month of Brand Watch.
5. **The bankers' association:** the shared platform.
6. **Associations of smaller institutions:** one deal, many members.
7. **Merchants:** the registry and Brand Watch.

**Public reach** comes from shared reports, literacy programmes and regulators' awareness campaigns, without fear-based messaging.

## 13. Roadmap

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Now** | Reviewed record, public check, first version of the API and console |
| **Months 1–6** | Regulator partnerships, MTN and bank pilots, data protection registration, WhatsApp Cloud API |
| **Months 7–12** | Shared layer with real partners, payment-screen warnings, USSD, paying customers |
| **Month 13 onward** | Licensed firms, a wider market in Ghana, then Nigeria and Kenya. Extend the merchant registry so people can check whether a contact or offer was listed by the company |

**Why it lasts after licensing:** schemes don't apply for licences, licensed names become worth impersonating, and regulators will always move more slowly than schemes.

## 14. Main risks

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Institutions won't share data | Give value first, then founding-partner terms, credits and legal cover |
| False reports hurt real businesses | Crowd reports are never public; independent sources, human review, appeals |
| Defamation or advice claims | Evidence only, no scores, legal review |
| Thin payment data before partnerships | Start with checks and forwarded offers, and prioritise MTN |
| Slow bank sales | Start with smaller firms and sandbox firms; grants fund early work |
| Ghana is a small market | Each new country is a data pack and a legal review |

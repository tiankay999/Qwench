# Qwench Sources and Q&A

Short answers for the panel on Tuesday, and where every figure comes from. Everyone should give the same answers.

## Q&A

Say these in your own words, but keep the facts the same. Agree who answers each topic so two people don't talk over each other. MTN, banks and regulators are prospects, so never imply a partnership.

### The basics

**What is Qwench, in one sentence?**

Ghana's fraud bureau: before a payment goes through, a bank or MoMo app checks the recipient against schemes regulators have named and fraud institutions have confirmed.

**Is this about virtual assets or all fraud?**

We start with investment and virtual asset schemes, because regulators publish notices on them and they collect money through MoMo and bank accounts. The same record then works for impersonation and other payment fraud.

**What's actually built?**

The WhatsApp check is live. The reviewed record is in progress, and the API and console are in build. The shared layer, cases, merchant registry and Brand Watch come next.

**Why no app?**

People won't download an app to check a number. Qwench works in the chats they already use, and inside the banking and MoMo apps they already have.

### Why institutions would buy

**Why would a bank pay for this?**

Every fraud case costs a bank in calls, investigations, disputes and customers who leave. That raises its cost to serve. Stopping the payment first means the case never reaches the contact centre.

**Doesn't MTN already do this? Why won't MTN build it?**

MTN can build for MTN, and it already takes reports through 419. But Telecel, AT and the banks won't share fraud data with their biggest competitor. A shared system needs a neutral operator, which is why we want MTN as a founding partner.

**We already have a fraud system. Why add this?**

Your system watches your own transactions. It can't see the offer behind a payment, or what's happening on other networks. Like a credit bureau, we feed your system and you make the decision.

**Why would an institution share its data with you?**

They get value first, through the regulator record and the feed. Then founding partners share only what schemes use, such as numbers and wallets, and never customer records. They get a governance seat and lower fees, and they get back more than they give.

**Why be first?**

It's like the move to chip cards. The first institutions set the standard, and others follow once it shows results. "The bank that warns you before you're scammed" is also a good story to tell customers.

**Won't warnings add friction for customers?**

Only when there's a match. Most payments are untouched, so normal customers see no extra step. For a large payment to a new recipient, the bank can call to confirm.

### Data and trust

**Where does the data come from?**

Today: public regulator notices, foreign regulators' lists, and what people check or forward. After partnerships: fraud numbers that operators and banks have confirmed. Regulator notices rarely list payment numbers, so partners are essential.

**Is it real time?**

The check answers in real time. The record updates when a regulator publishes or a partner confirms, and each fact is reviewed by a person first.

**What if people mass-report a business they dislike?**

Public reports can't trigger a warning. They only make us look. We count independent sources, not volume, and a person reviews everything. Registered merchants are never flagged automatically, and anyone flagged can appeal.

**What if you're wrong about a company?**

Every fact shows its source and date, so a mistake can be traced and corrected. There's an appeal route with a response deadline. We report evidence and don't give verdicts, and the institution decides what to do.

**Why no risk score?**

A score is our opinion, and it could count as investment advice or be defamatory. A bank's compliance team also can't defend a score. It can defend "named in this SEC notice on this date".

**How do you find schemes on TikTok and Telegram?**

People forward posts to us, and we review public posts by hand or through official channels. We don't scrape, and we never read private chats.

**Do you call suspected numbers to test them?**

No. We never contact suspects, because investigation belongs to the authorities. When concern passes a threshold, we open a case and send the evidence to them.

**What does AI do?**

It reads forwarded messages and pulls out names, numbers and links. It doesn't decide what's fraud. Only the reviewed record does that.

**Can't a scammer just look themselves up?**

Yes, and they'll see only what is already public: regulator and sanctions records, legal states and reviewed impersonation. What partners have confirmed stays private, and a number with hidden reports looks exactly like a number with none. If they change number, a forwarded offer links the new one back to the same scheme.

**Can a flyer trick your AI?**

It can try, and we test for it. The AI only reads and has no tools, so it can't act on anything. Status, tier and wording come from reviewed records and fixed templates, so it has no way to change a result. Hidden text never feeds a check, and text aimed at AI becomes a warning sign in the reply.

**Why won't you just say if it's safe?**

Because nobody can honestly promise that from the outside, and a wrong "safe" could cost someone their savings. We tell you how much evidence there is, from "Regulator warning" to "Not enough to go on", and show exactly why. It isn't a final ruling, and anyone named can appeal.

### Law and regulators

**How do you handle data protection?**

We store what was checked, not who checked it. Partners share scheme details, never customers, and matching uses keyed hashes under signed agreements. We'll register with the Data Protection Commission under Act 843 before public launch.

**Do you need a licence?**

We hold no money, so we don't expect to need a payment licence. We give no advice, which keeps us clear of the advice rules in Act 1154. We'll confirm the position with the SEC and CSA and have a Ghanaian lawyer review it before launch.

**Where does the SEC fit? Have they endorsed you?**

No, they haven't, and regulators wouldn't endorse a product like this. They're our top source. We ask for machine-readable registers and recognition as a data-sharing partner. In return, they get an early signal and their notices reach the point of payment.

**What happens once firms are licensed?**

The need grows. Schemes don't apply for licences, licensed names become worth impersonating, and regulators will always move more slowly than schemes.

**Is it legal to scan for fake listings for Brand Watch?**

Yes, if it's done properly. We use public content and official APIs, act on the brand owner's authority, and report through each platform's own takedown process.

### Competition

**Can't people just Google it?**

Google answers well for known names. It's weak on new schemes and MoMo numbers, it gives no audit trail, and it can even surface fake numbers. It also can't run inside a payment.

**What about Chainalysis or World-Check?**

They complement us. Chainalysis traces activity on the blockchain, and Qwench covers what happens off it. Based on what we found, World-Check has little Ghana-specific notice data.

**What about Truecaller?**

It relies on crowd labels and contact books, which is different data. We don't use it as a source. It could become a customer for confirmed scheme numbers.

**What if the bankers' association or Bank of Ghana builds this?**

That's the real risk. Our response is to offer Qwench as the investment-offer and public layer of that platform. Our public check shows what people are asking about, which a bank-only system doesn't see.

### Money

**How do you make money if the check is free?**

The public check gives reach and signal. Institutions pay for the API, feed and console. About 85% of projected revenue comes from institutions.

**Where do your prices come from?**

They're our assumptions, set below global benchmarks and to be tested in pilots. Nobody has agreed to pay them yet.

**Ghana's market looks small. Why invest?**

Ghana is about GH₵13.4M a year on our assumptions, and that's the starting point. Each new country is a data pack and a legal review, with Nigeria and then Kenya next.

**How do you know an offer really came from a company?**

We don't call an offer legitimate, and we won't. Today, a business can register its official numbers, and we can say whether a number is one of them. Next, companies could list their offers too. If the contact doesn't match, we say so and show the source.

**Can someone pay to remove a warning?**

No. Nobody can pay for a better result or hide a warning. Correcting a record goes through the appeal route, and only on evidence.

# Lean canvas

The submitted slide uses the hackathon's template, kept outside this repo. Prices and projections are assumptions.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | When someone pays a fraudster, the money is almost always gone. Banks recovered only about 5% of fraud losses in 2025. Regulators publish warnings, but customers and banks don't see them at the moment of paying. Each bank or MoMo network only sees its own fraud. |
| **Solution** | A check that runs before a payment goes through. If the recipient is linked to known fraud, the customer is warned. |
| **Key Metrics** | Banks and MoMo operators signed up. Warnings shown to customers. How early we spot new fraud. |
| **Unique Value Proposition** | Know who you're paying, before the money moves. Qwench is like a credit bureau for fraud. It puts regulator warnings and fraud confirmed by banks and MoMo operators in one place. Other tools watch one company's own transactions. Qwench works across networks and checks before the money leaves. Normal payments see no extra step. |
| **Unfair Advantage** | Neutral: rival banks and networks can share data through us, but not with each other. Ghana data checked by people. |
| **Channels** | Direct sales, starting with MTN and one bank. Banking associations. Free WhatsApp and Telegram checks. |
| **Customer Segments** | Banks and MoMo operators want fewer fraud cases and lower costs. Payment and virtual asset firms want to protect customers without a big fraud team. Merchants want to stop people faking their numbers. The public and regulators use it free. |
| **Cost Structures** | GH₵1.5M for the first 12 months: team and engineering 45%, checking data 20%, legal and data protection 15%, pilots with MTN and one bank 10%, Nigeria data 10%. |
| **Revenue Streams** | Yearly subscriptions: banks GH₵100,000–250,000, MoMo operators GH₵500,000, payment firms GH₵60,000–150,000. Monthly plans: virtual asset firms GH₵3,000, merchants GH₵200–500, investor alerts GH₵30. Prices are estimates. |
